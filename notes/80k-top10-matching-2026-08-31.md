## Method

The 80,000 Hours board is a redirector — every listing points at the employer's
own ATS. So "scraping the board" is the wrong unit of work. Each of the ten was
resolved to its **live source posting** (Greenhouse, Lever, the org's own
careers page, or a trade board), and the facts below come from that posting,
not from the 80k summary.

Recruiter matching was then run against the resolved facts — sector, seniority
band, salary, geography, engagement type — rather than against the job title.

## Three jobs got no recruiters, deliberately

You asked for five per job. Three cannot honestly carry five, or any.

**`20723` Summer Fellowship, Institute for Law & AI — zero.** A stipended
10-week programme for law students. Pitching a recruiter on this signals you
did not read the listing.

**`20675` Entrepreneur-in-Residence, GovAI — zero.** The "hire" is someone with
their own AI-governance startup idea who wants twelve months of runway and
$150k of seed funding. Selection is a two-page pitch. No agency maintains a
pipeline of people with a specific idea they want to found. There is no search
to run.

**`20698` Team Member, Lab Engagement, Encode — zero.** Junior (1–4 years),
"undergraduate degree or less," at a 1–10 person nonprofit, closing **4 Sep**.
No fee survives that combination, and the window is four days.

`20743` (NYT) carries three rather than five: the category is genuinely thin,
and see the caveat below.

## Per-job caveats that change the pitch

**`20684` CAIS — they are paying $1,500 for referrals.** The posting carries a
$1,500 referral bonus. A 20% fee on this role is $24,000–32,000. You are
pitching against an incentive they have already priced at one twentieth of
that. Lead with candidates, not with a fee conversation.

**`20686` Humans in Control — the three best-fitting recruiters are politically
wrong.** NRG, Movement Talent and Blueline Fellows are the strongest functional
matches: they place digital campaigners running email and SMS programmes, at
this exact band. All three are explicitly *progressive*. HIC is explicitly
nonpartisan and needs someone who can write one email that lands with "a
Republican small-business owner in Iowa and a Democratic parent in New
Hampshire." That is the single attribute they care most about, and it is the
one attribute these networks cannot supply cleanly. The two apolitical
nonprofit staffing firms rank lower on function and higher on fit.

Note also: my first search for this category returned Targeted Victory,
Mothership Strategies, Momentum Campaigns and Goodman Campaigns. **Those are
campaign agencies, not recruiters** — they run the programmes, they do not
staff them. They are excluded, and should not be pitched.

**`20674` GovAI — the deadline is 13 September, thirteen days out.** Too late
to stand up a search. Treat this as the relationship opener for their next
round, not a live mandate. GovAI runs biannual cohorts and was hiring a Head of
US Policy in July, so the cadence is predictable.

**`20743` NYT — prestigious and almost certainly unwinnable.** The role is
NewsGuild-represented and the posting asks for a resume, cover letter and 5–7
clips submitted directly. Newsrooms do not pay contingency fees for
correspondents. Included because you asked for the first ten, graded honestly.

## Where the money actually is

Two of the ten justify real effort:

**`20764` AI Cyber Red Teamer, Trajectory Labs.** The essential criteria are
"successful jailbreaks of frontier models" plus offensive security depth — CTF
results, published CVEs, bug bounty history. RedBlue Security's own marketing
lists ex-TAO members, Pwn2Own winners, PPP and DEF CON CTF winners and
kernel-level exploit developers. That is not an approximate match; it is the
same population. Code Red Partners is SF-based, security-exclusive since 2017,
and was live on an offensive-security search four days ago. Highest-confidence
pairing in the whole exercise.

**`20741` MATS Residency.** Up to 30 hires a year at $155,000–$285,000, an
explicitly thin pool, and a stated problem of competing with frontier lab
compensation. Impact Ops names that exact problem — benchmarking nonprofit comp
against frontier lab offers — as something they do. Thayon places researchers
out of DeepMind, FAIR, Cohere and Nvidia. Caveat: MATS runs this as a
structured application round closing 31 Oct, which reduces agency appetite.

## Supply-side note

23 distinct firms across 33 matches. Two clusters carry most of the weight:

- **Cyber** is a mature, well-populated recruiting market. Finding five
  credible offensive-security firms took one search.
- **AI safety** is not. There is no established executive-search firm for it.
  What exists is a handful of very small outfits (Impact Ops, Altruistic
  Careers, High Impact Recruitment — the latter two are one-person operations)
  plus frontier-AI generalists (Thayon, DeepRec.ai) reaching in from commercial
  AI. Several of the organisations that *look* like recruiters in this space —
  Talos Network, ERA, Orion, SteadRise — are talent *programmes* that place
  their own fellows, not agencies you can instruct. That gap is the actual
  market observation from this exercise.
