# Characters and Names in Butler's Odyssey

Butler renders the epic with **Roman names for the gods** and his own spellings
for several mortals. Any search of the text files must use Butler's forms —
searching for "Odysseus", "Athena", or "Zeus" finds nothing.

## Name map: Butler's form ↔ the familiar Greek form

| Butler writes | Greek name readers may expect |
|---|---|
| Ulysses | Odysseus |
| Jove | Zeus |
| Minerva | Athena / Athene |
| Neptune | Poseidon |
| Mercury | Hermes |
| Diana | Artemis |
| Venus | Aphrodite |
| Mars | Ares |
| Vulcan | Hephaestus |
| Juno | Hera |
| Saturn (as in "son of Saturn") | Cronus |
| Proserpine | Persephone |
| Teiresias | Tiresias |
| Euryclea | Eurycleia |
| Cicons | Cicones |
| Laestrygones | Laestrygonians |

Note the exceptions: Butler keeps the Greek **Hades** (the place and its god),
**Apollo**, and **Aeolus** unchanged, and writes the dawn goddess simply as
"Dawn" ("the child of morning, rosy-fingered Dawn"), not Aurora or Eos.

## Who's who

**The household of Ulysses (Ithaca)**
- **Ulysses** — king of Ithaca; the "ingenious hero" of the invocation.
- **Penelope** — his wife; holds off the suitors for years (the weaving trick).
- **Telemachus** — their son, a baby when Ulysses left, now coming of age.
- **Laertes** — Ulysses' aged father, living out of town on his farm (Book XXIV).
- **Euryclea** — old nurse; recognizes Ulysses by the boar-scar on his thigh (Book XIX).
- **Eumaeus** — the loyal swineherd; hosts the disguised Ulysses (Book XIV).
- **Philoetius** — loyal stockman; helps in the fight (Books XX–XXII).
- **Melanthius** — treacherous goatherd, sides with the suitors; punished in Book XXII.
- **Argus** — Ulysses' old hunting dog; recognizes him and dies (Book XVII).

**The suitors (over a hundred; the named leaders)**
- **Antinous** — the most brutal; first to die in Book XXII.
- **Eurymachus** — the smooth talker; second to die.
- **Amphinomus** — the least bad; warned by Ulysses in Book XVIII, killed anyway.
- **Irus** — not a suitor but their pet beggar; boxes Ulysses in Book XVIII.
- **Eupeithes** — Antinous' father; leads the failed revenge in Book XXIV.

**The journey (Books IX–XII, told in flashback)**
- **Polyphemus** — the Cyclops, son of Neptune; blinded by "Noman" (Book IX). His curse explains Neptune's grudge.
- **Aeolus** — keeper of the winds (Book X).
- **Circe** — enchantress of Aeaea; turns men to pigs, later helps (Books X, XII).
- **Teiresias** — blind Theban prophet consulted among the dead (Book XI).
- **Anticlea** — Ulysses' mother, met among the dead (Book XI).
- **Elpenor** — youngest crewman; dies falling off Circe's roof, begs for burial (Books X–XII).
- **Eurylochus** — second-in-command; cautious at Circe's, but urges eating the cattle of the Sun (Book XII).
- **Calypso** — goddess of Ogygia; keeps Ulysses seven years (Book V).
- **Scylla and Charybdis** — six-headed monster and whirlpool flanking the strait (Book XII).

**The Phaeacians (Scheria, Books VI–XIII)**
- **Alcinous** — king; hears Ulysses' tale and sends him home.
- **Arete** — his queen, whose favour matters most.
- **Nausicaa** — their daughter; finds the shipwrecked Ulysses.
- **Demodocus** — blind court bard; his songs make Ulysses weep (Book VIII).

**Pylos and Sparta (Books III–IV, XV)**
- **Nestor** — aged king of Pylos, Trojan-war veteran.
- **Menelaus and Helen** — at Sparta; recount Troy and the tale of Proteus.
- **Pisistratus** — Nestor's son, Telemachus' travel companion.
- **Theoclymenus** — fugitive seer Telemachus brings home; prophesies the suitors' doom (Book XX).

**Gods driving the plot**
- **Minerva** — Ulysses' constant protector; opens the poem by pleading his case.
- **Neptune** — his persecutor, avenging Polyphemus.
- **Jove** — arbiter between them.
- **Mercury** — messenger: orders Calypso to release Ulysses (Book V), gives Ulysses the herb moly against Circe (Book X).
