## What we built and the approach
**Angle:** `work-first` — build the deliverable before first contact, show
a sample, ask for judgment. **Asset:** two-page Target Account Map PDF
(`outreach/assets/sixsense-target-account-map.pdf`) — Tier 1 = four live
India plants with 16 individually verified decision-makers; Tier 2 = five
paths from existing wins (Wavetek→UMC, CG Semi→Renesas, PSMC↔Dholera);
Tier 3 = 19 expansion lookalikes with live triggers. **Asset offered, not
attached** — the ask is a micro-yes.

Copy went through 8 revisions. What each cut taught (now standing rules):
1. Never tell her what she's thinking ("pipeline is on your mind" — cut).
2. Never explain her market back to her ("a list this short gets won by
   name" — cut; only describe what WE did).
3. Limit the data, not ourselves ("built from the outside" read as a
   confession of inexperience — replaced with "all public signals").
4. Show the moat, not the names (name-roster paragraph replaced with the
   paths between accounts; said out loud "the names are the easy part").
5. Ask for judgment, not permission ("Am I going in the right direction?"
   sounded like a student; final: "Happy to send the whole thing. Mostly
   I'd like to know if you'd tier it the same way.").
6. No P.S. — two attempts at a personal note both read as manufactured;
   warmth belongs in email two, after she engages.
7. 7th-grade reading level; the registers survive simplification.
8. Deliberately no "I'm an hour from Vemagal" — proximity is a
   candidate's argument, not a partner's.

Distribution decision: NOT Instantly (July mass data: 395 sends → ~0.6%
positive; a founder clocks cold-email tooling instantly). One clean email
from the real Gmail, schedule-send to her morning.

## Panel experiment — 30 Aug (reverted)

Ran a 21-agent workflow (5 constructions x 3 judge lenses + synthesis) to
rewrite the email against six criteria. 934k tokens. Arun's verdict: the
previous version was better. Reverted to v8.

**Why it failed.** The rewrite optimised the criteria and lost the email.
It went telegraphic ("Four names each", "So are UMC's specialty fabs, off
Wavetek"), dropped the actual offer to send the map, and re-committed the
banned sin in a subtler form — "Your August post put the $120B after the
defect is found, not in finding it" explains her own argument back to her.
Judges scoring criteria did not catch that, because it scored well on
every criterion.

**Lesson: a rubric is not a reader.** Scoring copy against a checklist
optimises for the checklist. Cheaper and better: one careful pass plus
Arun's own ear, which has caught every real problem in this email so far
(scripted, fake, expert-ish, glued, vague) faster than any panel.
