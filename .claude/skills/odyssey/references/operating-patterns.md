# Operating Patterns

Five recurring failure-and-decision shapes, each named after an episode in the
poem, each with the passage it comes from and the place it has already shown up
in this repo's own work.

Why bother: these names decay into vibes if you only half-remember them. Most
people say "Sirens" and mean "temptation," which is useless advice. The text
says something sharper and actionable. Each pattern below cites its file so the
precise version stays reachable.

Use these **inward** — for naming failure modes, tagging postmortems, and
sharpening a decision at the moment you make it. Never as borrowed authority in
customer-facing copy. Quoting old wisdom to sound credible is the same move as
an unevidenced proof number: impressive, unearned. See the README's rule 3.

---

## 1. The Sirens — bind yourself before the pressure arrives

**Text:** Book XII (`text/book-12.md`)

Odysseus knows a song ahead will compromise his judgment. He does not resolve
to resist it. While still calm, he has the crew tie him to the mast and adds the
decisive clause: if he begs to be released, they are to "bind me more tightly
still." He also seals the crew's ears with wax, so they cannot be argued into
freeing him.

Two layers matter here. He constrains his future self, *and* he removes his
future self's ability to countermand the constraint.

**The rule:** A rule you can talk yourself out of in the moment is not a rule.
Write constraints while calm, and put enforcement somewhere your later self does
not control.

**Already here:** The README's five rules for outbound copy, especially rule 5,
"A draft is not a send." Those were written by a clear-headed self to govern a
pressured one. The layer not yet built is the wax: an agent that holds the rule
even when instructed later to relax it.

## 2. Scylla and Charybdis — take the bounded loss on purpose

**Text:** Book XII (`text/book-12.md`)

Circe's counsel is directional, not a shrug about two bad options. Steer close
to Scylla, she says, because "you had better lose six men than your whole crew."
Odysseus immediately asks whether he can avoid both dangers. He cannot. That
question — *can I escape the tradeoff entirely* — is the trap, and it costs
time that the decision does not have.

**The rule:** When the real choice is a known bounded loss against a possible
unbounded one, take the bounded loss deliberately and early. Refusing to choose
is itself a choice, usually the unbounded one.

**Already here:** `outreach/warm-revival-2026-08-29.md` — deleting roughly 20
July drafts rather than sending them late. The known loss is six weeks of
written work. The unbounded loss would be damaging the two relationships that
are actually live. That call was made correctly; this pattern names why, so the
next one is easier.

## 3. The cattle of the Sun — how a team drifts once no one is watching

**Text:** Book XII (`text/book-12.md`)

The strongest of the five, and sharper in the text than in most retellings.
There is an explicit prohibition against touching the herd. Odysseus makes every
crewman swear an oath. Then he sleeps. Under real pressure — famine — Eurylochus
offers a rationalization: take the cattle now, and build the god a fine temple
afterward to settle it. "Thus spoke Eurylochus, and the men approved his words."
The ship is lost.

The full shape: explicit rule, sworn agreement, supervisor absent, genuine
pressure, a plausible *we'll make it right later*, unanimous drift, catastrophe.
Note that nobody in the story is a villain. They are hungry, and the argument
sounds reasonable.

**The rule:** Constraints fail under pressure when the supervisor is away, and
they fail through reasonable-sounding rationalization rather than open defiance.
Watch for "we'll substantiate it later" — that sentence is the tell.

**Already here:** This is exactly what produced the retired July claims — "20
placements in 55 days" and "$105K in a single quarter" — and the stale drafts
naming an unverified "seven-figure defense R&D award." No rule was openly
broken. *We'll prove it later* just felt fine at the time. Any agent drafting
copy is a crew member; assume this failure mode, and check for it.

## 4. Penelope's bed — proof the other side can check without trusting you

**Text:** Book XXIII (`text/book-23.md`)

Penelope has heard a long parade of strangers arrive with convincing stories
about her husband. So when the real one appears, she does not ask for a better
story — stories can be fabricated, and she has been burned by them. She hints
that their bed has been moved. Odysseus corrects her indignantly, because he
built it around a living olive tree and it cannot be moved. The proof works
because it is structurally impossible to fake, not because it is persuasive.

**The rule:** A claim the recipient can verify independently beats a stronger
claim they must take on faith. Design proof so it does not route through trust
you have not yet earned.

**Already here:** The inventory is built on this. Every role carries a primary
source link "so the recipient can verify independently," plus a band, a
freshness date, and an honest note when a number is an estimate rather than
company-stated. That is the bed test, and it is the actual product
differentiator — every prospect's inbox is Penelope's hall.

## 5. Nausicaa and Alcinous — size the ask to the ground you have prepared

**Text:** Book VI and Book IX (`text/book-06.md`, `text/book-09.md`)

Shipwrecked, naked, with no standing whatsoever, Odysseus meets Nausicaa. What
he actually needs is passage home. What he asks for is directions to the town
and something to wrap around himself — and he opens by wishing her a husband and
a happy home. He asks for far less than he wants, from a position of weakness.

Later, at Alcinous's court, he withholds his name for several books, earns the
hospitality first, and only then says plainly: "I am Ulysses son of Laertes."
The large ask — a ship, an escort, treasure — lands after the ground is
prepared, not before.

**The rule:** The size of the ask should track the ground you have actually
prepared, not the size of your need. Identity and disclosure are levers to spend
deliberately, not formalities to rush through.

**Already here:** The Peter Heyer approach in
`outreach/warm-revival-2026-08-29.md` — apologize once, cleanly, do *not* ask
for the rescheduled call, lead with the hardest verified search, and give him an
explicit cost-free way to say no. That is the Nausicaa move: after 53 days of
silence there is no ground, so the ask shrinks to almost nothing.

## Using these day to day

Tag decisions and postmortems with the pattern name. "That's a cattle-of-the-Sun
failure" carries the whole scenario — rule existed, nobody watching, real
pressure, comfortable rationalization — in four words. Over a month, the tags
show which failure actually keeps recurring, which is worth more than any single
diagnosis.
